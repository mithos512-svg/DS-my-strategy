# NameCard: The broader initiative — bringing the team into systems thinking

## Note on applying F.18 here

Checked against the real specification (fpf.sh/generated/patterns/F.18) after
finding the first two NameCards used only a seminar-simplified version of the
mantra. The real F.18 is a formal ontological-naming protocol for FPF-internal
governed values (roles, relations, methods — with fields like `ReferenceScheme`,
`ClaimGraph`, `BridgeRefs`, `PublicRowStatus`), written for ontology editors,
not for naming a business initiative. This card is an explicit **practical
adaptation** of F.18's Solution steps (1–5, matching the real spec) to a
non-formal naming decision — not a literal application of the full protocol.
No FPF `GoverningPatternRef` genuinely applies here; noted as such rather than
forced.

## Step 1 — Recover the governed value

What is actually being named: the **ongoing organizational effort to build
systems-thinking capability in the team over time** — the goal-level
initiative. This is distinct from any single session format. "Systems
Thinking Workshop" (see namecard.md) names one *mechanism* serving this goal;
the goal itself has not yet been named on its own.

Kind (informal, no strict FPF U-kind applies): an internal capability-building
initiative / program — closest loose analogy is `U.Capability` (A.2.2, the
ability being built) plus `C.20`/`C.21` (composition and health of a
discipline), but neither is a literal governing pattern for a business
initiative name.

## Step 2 — Is a durable name needed?

Yes. The initiative spans months, will be referenced in planning documents
and possibly in reporting to leadership, and needs to stay recognizable as
one continuing effort independent of which specific session format is used
at any given time (workshops now, possibly other formats later).

## Step 3 — Local sense

Who reads it: the user (planning, current/WeekPlan and current/Strategy-*),
possibly leadership (if the initiative is reported as a team-development
effort). For what work: referring to the overarching goal in a way that
stays stable even if the session mechanism changes.

## Step 4 — Candidate set (at least two live head-term families)

| Family | Candidate |
|---|---|
| Program / structured effort | **Systems Thinking Program** |
| Capability-building | **Systems Thinking Capability Building** |
| Skill uplift (tech-org jargon) | Systems Thinking Uplift |
| Adoption | Systems Thinking Adoption |
| Culture change | Systems Thinking Culture Building |
| Literacy | Systems Thinking Literacy Program |

## Step 5 — Comparison, rejected candidates, rationale

- **Systems Thinking Adoption** — rejected. "Adoption" reads as a one-time
  threshold event (like adopting a tool), not an ongoing capability-building
  process. Wrong framing for a multi-session, multi-month effort.
- **Systems Thinking Culture Building** — rejected. Overclaims: a workshop
  series builds individual/team *skill*, not organizational *culture*. Sets
  an expectation the initiative can't credibly deliver on at this scale.
- **Systems Thinking Uplift** — rejected. Familiar in security/tech-org
  contexts ("security uplift") but risks being unclear jargon outside that
  specific circle; not safe to assume the whole team reads it the same way.
- **Systems Thinking Literacy Program** — close contender. "Literacy" is
  accurate (basic working competence, not mastery) but slightly undersells
  the applied, case-based nature of the sessions — sounds more like reading
  comprehension than hands-on pattern application.
- **Systems Thinking Capability Building** — strong candidate. Precise: names
  the actual target (a capability, not a culture or a one-time event). Matches
  the loose `U.Capability` analogy from Step 1. Slightly long for repeated use
  in tables/plans.
- **Systems Thinking Program** — selected. Standard, unambiguous corporate
  term for a structured, ongoing, multi-session effort. Short enough for
  repeated use. Doesn't overclaim (unlike "culture") and doesn't undersell
  (unlike "literacy"). Reads correctly to both technical and non-technical
  audiences without needing the word explained.

## Decision

- **TechLabel:** *Systems Thinking Capability-Building Program* (for
  formal/planning contexts where precision about the target — capability,
  not culture — matters)
- **PlainLabel:** **Systems Thinking Program** (for everyday reference,
  planning docs, and any reporting to others)

## Relation to the other two NameCards

- **Systems Thinking Program** (this card) — the goal-level initiative.
- **Systems Thinking Workshop** (namecard.md) — one session-level mechanism
  serving that program; not the only possible one.
- **Applied Systems Thinking Session** (namecard-tech-label.md) — the
  TechLabel for that same session mechanism, for formal documents.

Parent–child, not synonyms: the Program is *why*; the Workshop is *how*,
*for now*.

## Reopen condition

Reopen if the initiative's actual mechanism diversifies beyond sessions
(e.g. adds asynchronous material, a reading list, 1:1 coaching) enough that
"Program" starts to feel too narrow or the wrong register — or if leadership
or the team consistently uses a different term in practice, in which case
local usage should win over this card's selection.
